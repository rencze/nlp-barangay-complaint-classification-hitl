\chapter*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This study develops a multilingual three-class text-classification component for a proposed barangay complaint-reporting system. A controlled synthetic research corpus of 24,000 narratives covers 60 Valid complaint topics, 28 Suspicious clarification reasons, 30 Spam submission types, and four assigned language groups. The 118 categories guide scenario construction and auditing, rather than serving as prediction classes. A TF--IDF and linear support vector machine (SVM) is compared with a Multinomial Naive Bayes baseline. A three-fold scenario-grouped grid search evaluated 18 SVM configurations. A separate five-fold grouped cross-validation on the training partition yielded mean Macro-F1 scores of 1.0000 for SVM and 0.99245 for Naive Bayes, with standard deviations of 0.00000 and 0.00097. On 2,399 validation records, SVM achieved 1.0000 Macro-F1 while Naive Bayes achieved 0.9921. The previously evaluated 2,388-record synthetic test partition yielded Macro-F1 scores of 1.0000 and 0.9916, respectively; these scores do not establish authentic-report generalization. Training-size learning curves, 15-epoch auxiliary stochastic-gradient hinge-loss curves, class-wise confusion matrices, language-group evaluation and feature-ablation diagnostics help interpret these scores. These observations characterize performance on researcher-generated messages and cannot independently establish reliability on previously unseen styles of citizen reporting. The study proposes meaning-preserving preprocessing, combined word/character features, probability-calibrated inference, administrator verification, accountable case management and controlled offline feedback as future operational components. These extensions have not been validated by the reported word-level SVM experiments and require distinct implementation and end-to-end testing. 
\par\medskip\textbf{Keywords:} barangay reporting; complaint classification; natural language processing; TF--IDF; support vector machine; controlled synthetic data; group cross-validation; human-in-the-loop.
